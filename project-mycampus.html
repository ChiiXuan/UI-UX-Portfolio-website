Case Study

MyCampus AR Navigation Map

Campus navigation can be confusing for new students and visitors, especially in multi-floor buildings. MyCampus AR Navigation Map is an AR wayfinding prototype that combines 2D route preview with turn-by-turn AR guidance. The design was developed through research, prototyping, and usability testing to improve clarity and confidence while navigating.

Project Overview

Objectives

Design an AR-based campus navigation experience that is interactive, intuitive, and trustworthy. The goal is to reduce confusion, reduce the time needed to find destinations, and improve overall navigation satisfaction for students, staff, and visitors.

Target Users

The primary users are new university students and campus visitors who frequently navigate large or unfamiliar campus environments. They often struggle with complex layouts and limited signage, especially at the start of the semester or during events.

Problem Space

Traditional printed maps and signboards are not interactive or real-time, making navigation time-consuming and stressful. Indoor navigation also faces challenges like signal limitations, multi-floor routing, and difficulty confirming “where am I” and “which way should I go” quickly while walking.

Design Process

Research

We conducted a survey-based research plan using Google Forms to identify campus navigation difficulties and explore how AR can improve navigation efficiency. Findings showed that navigation stress often happens during rush times (e.g., being late), and users strongly preferred AR arrows/labels and real-time assistance. However, users also raised concerns about device performance (battery drain/overheating/lag) and AR tracking reliability in dark or crowded areas.

Ideation

Based on the research themes, we ideated around “reliability and trust” during navigation. We focused on reducing cognitive load under time pressure (e.g., showing ETA/clear guidance), supporting different contexts (lighting/crowds), and improving indoor accuracy using cross-channel ideas such as QR “phygital” anchoring at building entrances.

Wireframing

We created low-fidelity paper prototype screens to quickly validate layout and user flow before moving into digital design. Key screens included Home Dashboard, Search, 2D Map Preview, AR Navigation cues, Safety Notice, Calibration, and Destination Arrival. This helped us identify missing elements early and refine the flow at low cost.

Prototyping

We structured the app into five main modules: Home Dashboard, Navigation Flow, AR Navigation Mode, Recovery & Support, and Help & Tutorial. The AR mode includes safety notice and calibration before entering the camera-based navigation, plus tracking checks and automatic rerouting to maintain continuity. Multi-story navigation is supported with stair/lift guidance.

Testing

We ran task-based usability tests to evaluate key flows such as locating stairs on the 2D map, login clarity (student vs guest), and browsing/searching in staff/facility directory. We observed user hesitation, confusion points, and collected feedback to drive refinements.

Design Artifacts

  • Research: Survey plan + key findings/themes (stress, AR demand, technical barriers).
  • PACT summary: People / Activities / Contexts / Technologies constraints for campus navigation.
  • Information architecture: 5 main modules + key screens.
  • Task flows: Select destination → route preview → safety + calibration → AR navigation → arrival.
  • Low-fidelity artifacts: Paper prototype screens (home, search, map preview, AR cues, safety, calibration, arrival).
  • Hi-fi prototype screenshots: Key screens for login, home, 2D map, AR navigation, directory.
  • Usability testing notes: Findings + iteration changes (before/after if available).

Challenges & Solutions

Challenge 1

Stair icon visibility on the 2D map

Users confused stairs/lift icons with wall and room markers; vertical navigation was hard to spot without zooming.

Solution

Increase icon contrast and hierarchy, add consistent stair/lift labels, and keep them visible at mid zoom levels.

Challenge 2

Student vs Guest login confusion

Primary and secondary actions looked too similar, leading to accidental taps and unclear entry paths.

Solution

Differentiate primary vs secondary buttons (“Sign In” vs “Guest Login”), clarify copy, and add inline validation states.

Challenge 3

Directory felt long and heavy

Users experienced cognitive overload scanning long lists with minimal grouping or filters.

Solution

Add category chips/filters at the top, restructure list groupings, and surface quick access to common destinations.

Results

Overall, testing showed that most users found the login process easy to follow and the AR navigation interface generally clear and understandable. However, usability testing also surfaced key improvement areas (stair icon clarity and directory browsing). After iteration, we improved map icon hierarchy, clarified login pathways, and optimized directory scanning with search filters to reduce confusion and effort.

My Role

This was a group project. My main contributions were:

  • Created the Wireframes / Paper Prototype Screens (low-fidelity screens + screen descriptions) to define structure and user flow early.
  • Contributed to Design Goal and High-Fidelity Prototype Screens in the later stage to support the final prototype direction.

Next Steps

  • Improve robustness in dark/crowded environments with adaptive contrast and guidance for tracking issues.
  • Optimize performance for lower-end devices (heat/battery).
  • Add optional voice guidance plus stronger real-time feedback (ETA cues) to lower cognitive load under time pressure.
  • Expand accessibility personalization.
  • Strengthen indoor accuracy using QR-based anchoring at entrances and key decision points.
